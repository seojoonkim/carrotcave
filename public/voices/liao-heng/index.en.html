CARROT CAVE INSIGHTS

  1. As Moore’s Law loses momentum, performance comes not from process technology but from the ability to co-design chips, memory, networks, compilers, and models.
  2. Supply-chain constraints can be more than a cost: they can force organizations to relearn the underlying technologies that an external division of labor had kept out of view.
  3. In an inference-driven market, metrics that determine the economics of the service as a whole—such as memory bandwidth, communication latency, and token throughput—matter more than peak FLOPS.
  4. Open source is not a declaration of goodwill but an industrial mechanism that brings real-world feedback into the design process and broadens the perspective of closed organizations.
  5. Chip talent develops through repeated tape-outs and accountability for failure rather than lectures, so long-term competitiveness accumulates through an organization’s experience of seeing projects through to completion.

Core Ideas Running Through the Interview

  1. Defining the problem correctly comes before finding the answer. If you simply accept the incumbents’ metrics and answers, you miss solutions suited to your own constraints.
  2. Co-design across every layer matters more than local optimization. Chips, memory, networks, compilers, models, power, and cooling compensate for one another’s weaknesses to deliver overall throughput.
  3. Constraints force you to look at the layers below. Drawing on his own experience, Liao Heng argues that supply chain disruptions forced Huawei to learn about the equipment, processes, and components that the global division of labor had kept out of view.
  4. Technical feasibility and industrial sustainability are different things. What is needed is not prototypes or benchmarks, but a closed loop of reliable mass production, customer value, profits, and reinvestment.
  5. Talent develops through long feedback cycles and crossing disciplinary boundaries. Both the depth gained through repeated tape-outs and the breadth needed to translate between different layers are essential.
  6. AI competition is shifting from individual chips to systems and ecosystems. HBM, optical interconnects, megakernels, open source, and a critical mass of developers matter as much as peak compute performance.
  7. Optimism about China is distinguished from zero-sum nationalism. The interview concludes with an insider’s bet on the long-term potential of Chinese industry; it does not present other countries’ decline as either a fact or a goal.

  • Lingqu (灵衢): Huawei’s proposed high-speed interconnect architecture.

  • 2.5D, Stacking (堆叠), NPO: Paths to scaling through packaging and optical interconnects, rather than leading-edge process nodes alone.
  • Tape-out (流片): The stage at which a chip design is finalized as manufacturing data. Repeated experience with this process is presented as central to developing talent.
  • Economically Sustainable Closed Loop (经济上可循环 / 正向闭环): A state in which a supply chain continually generates profits and reinvestment.
